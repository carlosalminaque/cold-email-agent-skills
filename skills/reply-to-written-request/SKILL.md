# Reply to a written request

The reader has many replies to skim. Most get skipped because they missed an explicit ask, ignored the reader's stated worry, or sounded like a vendor guessing. This method avoids all three.

## 1. Read the request for three things

- **Explicit asks.** Numbered questions, "start your reply with the word X", length limits, required tools, "please provide" lists. Answer every one, in their order, with their numbering. A missed ask is the most common reason a reply is skipped.
- **Stated fears.** "Not for someone who simply...", "no scraped data", "real experience, not AI-written." The first paragraph answers the biggest fear before anything else.
- **What they are buying.** Hours of work, a built system, or an outcome. Describe the deliverable in their words, and define it if it is vague ("interested leads" means replies with intent, not contact rows).

- Valid: opening line answers "we've been burned by scraped lists" with how the list is actually built and checked.
- Invalid: opening line is "I have 5 years of experience and would love to help."

## 2. Structure

1. **Opening:** one paragraph answering the core fear with the specific method, not a claim of experience.
2. **"Your N questions:"** then each answered under its own number, one short paragraph each.
3. **The scenario, worked.** If the request describes a case ("find decision makers at companies like..."), work it briefly. Most replies skip this, and it is the part the reader studies closest.
4. **What to agree first:** one or two scoping questions that show judgment (which segment first, what counts as success, who sends from which account).
5. **Close:** offer proof that is cheap for them to check, such as a short video of the work running or a short call.

## 3. Honesty

- State a gap in the first line of the answer that has it, then the transferable specific.
  - Valid: "I haven't used Tool A. I use Tool B daily, which works the same way: ..."
  - Invalid: "Yes, experienced with Tool A."
- Never invent a number, a client name, a result or a skill level. If no result number exists yet, say so, give a process result instead, and offer the number later.
- Bracket anything only the sender can fill: "[State your language level plainly, or delete this line.]"
- Use only facts the sender has confirmed. If the fact list you are working from is more than about a month old, ask before using it.
- Describe who the sender works with in their public wording (their own site), never in internal labels used for sorting leads.

## 4. Brackets are a real risk

Someone may paste the whole reply without noticing a bracket. A reader who sees literal placeholder text reads it as unedited AI output and may report or discard it. Every time a draft contains a bracket, list each one in the message alongside the draft and say what happens if it goes out unfilled. Do not rely on the formatting alone.

- Valid: "Two brackets to fill before sending: language level (line 4), past client name (line 9). Sent unfilled, it reads as spam."
- Invalid: delivering the draft with brackets and no mention of them.

## 5. Price

Leave price out of the body unless the request explicitly asks for a written breakdown in the text. If the form has its own price or rate field, that field carries it. If asked whether to bid low, answer with evidence and note that the number is the sender's call.

## 6. Language checks (in code, not by eye)

- Count characters against the form's limit.
- Confirm any required first word is present.
- Search for the sender's banned words and for em dashes.
- Match the sender's real voice: first person and hands-on for one person; do not say "we" or "my team" if there is no team.

## 7. Reader-reaction check

Before delivering, read it as the person who wrote the request and write their one-line reaction. If it is "they skipped my question 4", "this is a vendor template" or "they're guessing", fix it. Show that reaction line above the draft so the sender can check the reading too.

## 8. Delivery

Paste-ready fenced blocks as the last thing in the message. State the verified character count and what was cut if a first draft ran over. If the form has separate boxes (a general box plus one per question), give one labeled block per box instead of one blended letter.
