# Campaign angles

Needs: none

The first campaign idea tends to become the only one. Weeks later the team is tuning subject lines on an angle nobody compared against alternatives. A short table of many ideas, built before anything is sourced or written, costs an hour and shows which ones the data can actually support. This skill ends at options and reasoning. It never launches anything.

## When

- Before the first campaign for a new market or offer.
- Before a new sourcing push, to avoid tunnel vision.
- When a live angle is underperforming and you need the next one to test.
- Not as a mandatory gate on every campaign.

## The one rule that shapes the table

Research decides who gets emailed and when. It never decides what the email opens with. A hire, a funding round, a launch or a line on their site can put a company on a list or time a send. The email itself does not mention it: no "I noticed you just..." opener.

## The eight methods

Work through each one; several can feed the same idea.

1. **Best-client pattern.** What do your five strongest clients have in common: industry, size, buyer title, what was happening when they bought? This gives the core filters.
2. **Their own words.** Mine reviews, testimonials, case studies and sales emails for phrases a prospect would recognize.
3. **Wins and losses.** Recent closed deals show what to lead with and which objections to meet early.
4. **The moment before they buy.** Which events come just before a company buys something like this? Use them to build and time a list. The email stays silent about the event.
5. **Where proof already exists.** A vertical, channel or client type with real results gives a narrow campaign that persuades on its own.
6. **What competitors all say.** Read five competitor sites, then find a position that sounds unlike them.
7. **An easy first yes.** A small, low-risk first step (a sample, a short audit, a teardown) is easier to accept than a call.
8. **Wide or narrow.** For each idea choose: every company that fits, or only those with a recent reason to buy. This sets list size and timing, never the email's content.

## Steps

1. **Read your one source** for who you target and how you qualify them. Every idea must still pass those gates. A narrow list changes who and when, not how carefully leads are qualified. This table is not qualification. Two scopes: for your own campaigns every lead clears the gates; if you run campaigns for others, the ideas come from that client's intake, still pass the same gates, and the client approves the chosen three on the brief in writing.
2. **Build the table.** 15 to 20 rows, columns: `# | Method | List filters | Wide or narrow | What the first email asks or offers | Sourceable?` See `references/table-example.md`.
3. **Keep every first email simple:** short, one easy question, one small useful offer, nothing about what was looked up.
4. **Check every filter can be sourced** with the tools you actually have. Drop any idea that needs a filter no tool can query, and don't stack more narrow filters than those tools handle.
5. **Check two objections separately.** Proof that prospects understand the category rules out an education asset only. It says nothing about whether they trust the provider, which a founder video or process walkthrough addresses.
6. **Mark your recommended three,** with one line of reasoning each, so they can go straight onto a one-page brief.
7. **Hand it over.** A human (or the client, for a client's campaign) picks (see `outreach-experiments` if volume allows a test). Sourcing and staging follow in `lead-qualification` and `campaign-staging`.

## Valid and Invalid

- Valid: "List: software firms with a sales post opened in the last 60 days (narrow). First email: one easy question and a sample offer. The post is never mentioned."
- Invalid: "Your last few job posts are all engineering and support, none in sales." (an opener built on a fact about the prospect)
- Valid: "Wide: every company inside the gates. Narrow: the same list cut to a recent reason to buy."
- Invalid: "Generic blast to everyone in the industry." (fails the qualification gates, whatever the list width)
- Invalid: a table of eight ideas, two of them needing data no tool returns.

## Output

The table, the marked three with their reasons, and a short note on which ideas are cheapest to source. No launch, no copy.
