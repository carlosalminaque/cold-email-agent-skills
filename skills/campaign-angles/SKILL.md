# Campaign angles

The first campaign idea tends to become the only one. Weeks later the team is tuning subject lines on an angle nobody compared against alternatives. A short table of many concepts, built before anything is sourced or written, costs an hour and shows which angles the data can actually support. This skill ends at options and reasoning. It never launches anything.

## When

- Before the first campaign for a new market or offer.
- Before a new sourcing push, to avoid tunnel vision.
- When a live angle is underperforming and you need the next one to test.
- Not as a mandatory gate on every campaign.

## Steps

1. **Read your one source** for who you target and how you qualify them. Every concept must still pass those gates. This table is not qualification.
2. **Keep two dials separate.** Breadth is how wide the list is. Signal depth is how much each lead's selection rests on evidence. Breadth can flex with market size. Signal depth never drops to zero.
3. **Use the three tiers.**
   - **Broad:** the widest list inside your gates, chosen on a firmographic fact you can check now (industry, role, a page on their site such as careers, team or pricing).
   - **Focused:** Broad plus exactly one dated trigger event (a hire, a funding round, a tech change).
   - **Niche:** several dated triggers stacked on each account. Small list, the most research per account.
4. **Size check.** Market size guides breadth (see `market-size-check`). A small market rarely supports a lighter, templated Broad tier. Only a very large one does.
5. **Build the table.** Columns: `# | Tier | List filters | Angle | Per-account research?` Order strictly Broad, then Focused, then Niche. See `references/table-example.md`.
6. **Include at least one no-research concept,** narrow enough that a fixed message is credible for every lead on it.
7. **Add one to three front-end offers:** the one sentence that makes an angle worth opening (for example, two sample emails written for their market).
8. **Check every filter can be sourced** with the tools you actually have. Drop any concept that needs a filter no tool can query.
9. **Test each research angle** for three things: it is grounded in published facts and claims nothing about their internal state; the fact and the ask are separate sentences; and the fact supports why they would need your service. True and specific is not enough.
10. **Hand it over.** A human picks one angle or a small A/B set (see `outreach-experiments`). Sourcing and staging follow in `lead-qualification` and `campaign-staging`.

## Valid and Invalid

- Valid research angle: "Your last few job posts are all engineering and support, none in sales." It supports the need for outbound help.
- Invalid: "Your pricing page has no self-serve plan." True, checkable, and unrelated to the service.
- Valid tier label: "Focused: one trigger, a sales hire posted in the last 60 days."
- Invalid: "Broad: no signal, generic blast to everyone in the industry."
- Invalid: a table of eight concepts, all Niche, two of them needing data no tool returns.

## Output

The table, the front-end offers, a short note on which concepts are cheapest to source, and your recommendation with one line of reasoning. No launch, no copy.
