# Example ideas table

A fictional table for an outbound service selling to small B2B software companies. Company names and numbers are invented. Your own filters come from your one source.

| # | Method | List filters | Wide or narrow | What the first email asks or offers | Sourceable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Best-client pattern | B2B software, 10 to 50 staff, your countries | Wide | Would two sample emails for your market be useful? | Yes |
| 2 | Their own words | Same, with reviews mentioning slow lead flow | Narrow | Offers a short teardown of their cold outreach | Yes |
| 3 | Wins and losses | Same, founder still doing the selling | Wide | Asks who handles new business today | Yes |
| 4 | Moment before they buy | Same, funding round in the last 12 months | Narrow | Sample emails; the round is never mentioned | Yes |
| 5 | Moment before they buy | Same, first sales post open | Narrow | Sample emails; the post is never mentioned | Yes |
| 6 | Where proof already exists | The one vertical with client results | Narrow | Asks if that vertical is a priority this quarter | Yes |
| 7 | What competitors all say | Same as 1 | Wide | A position no competitor site uses | Yes |
| 8 | An easy first yes | Same as 1 | Wide | A list of 20 named accounts that fit their best customer | Yes |

Recommended three: 1 (largest list that passes the gates), 4 (timed list, same simple email), 8 (easiest yes). A real table has 15 to 20 rows.
