# Approved reply send

Needs: browser control

A warm reply goes cold fast. The usual way it gets lost is not a bad draft but a buried one: the waiting reply sits at the bottom of a long status report and nobody sees it for a day. The second way is a closing line that asks the prospect something they just answered, or pushes a call they never asked for. The third is "sent" reported from a button click, when the email never left. This skill handles all three.

## When

- A real prospect reply needs a human's yes.
- The human says "send it" or approves a proposed reply.

## Part 1: flag it

1. Confirm it is real: open its own thread and the message itself, and cite the lead id and message time in the flag. A test, a bounce or an auto-responder is not a waiting reply. Flag as soon as it is found, but never cut the research short to do it; a reply still unanswered when you finish is the first line of your final summary.
   - Also search the sending mailbox's Sent folder for the prospect. A reply sent by hand outside the sending tool leaves the thread looking unanswered. Never flag a reply that already went out.
   - An opt-out or a bounce needs no research and no drafted reply. Confirm the removal and nothing more.
   - A message that asks nothing and only restates what is settled (for example, confirming a time already booked) is not waiting. Leave it answered and draft nothing.
2. Do the research first. Read the prospect's home, services, about and case-study pages, plus pricing if it is published, and your own knowledge base. Read their pages as raw text with collapsed FAQ answers opened, never through a fetch that summarizes. Check that anything the reply promises can actually be delivered, and find outside evidence for any price or term that departs from your knowledge base. If a drafting system produces proposals, start from its draft and send corrections back to it rather than writing a parallel one; draft yourself only when it is down, and say so in one line. Check anything the reply links to (for a calendar link, what it opens and the soonest slot).
3. Send the flag as its own message, before any other work, in this shape and nothing else:

```
REPLY WAITING ON YOU: <name>, <company>
They wrote (<time>): "<their words>"
Waiting: <how long>
Read: <each page and source by name; name any page not read and why>
Real question: <what they are really asking, read from the whole thread>
Reviewed: <how many review rounds, and that the last found nothing>

Proposed reply:
<the exact text>

New in this reply: <any fact, term or promise not already on record, or "nothing new">
```

Ask for the yes in the same turn as a structured decision (Yes and No options), never as a typed "say yes and I send it" line. Nothing comes after the flag; the one exception is a fact that changes whether they should approve, in one line just above the reply. Keep the proposed reply under about 120 words, and use this shape even when the human only shares a screenshot and asks how to answer.

## Part 2: check the draft

Samples, when offered: a yes to samples is answered with the samples, not a promise of them; open any pending draft already on the thread and say in one line how yours differs. Each sample follows your first-email rules (length, sentence cap, no opening on a found fact), uses the prospect's own named clients and terms as proof, promises no asset their site does not show, and names the fields it needs per prospect. Close by asking which sample is closer and what is off; if the reply runs past the length cap, say so on the "New in this reply" line.

The body:

- Answer in the prospect's own terms. If their message corrects an assumption ("actually..."), use their version everywhere.
- Lead with the direct answer, then apply it to their case. Answer every question asked. If what you offer differs from what they asked for, say so in the first sentence.
- Plain words only: no jargon or terms the reader must look up; say what a thing does. Never refer to an earlier email by its time alone; say what it said.
- "Send me info": one sentence on the offer, the priced terms, and a concrete yes-or-no offer. A link alone is never the reply. "Next steps": one sentence, then the two or three steps with what each costs, then the application link once you have opened it.
- They name a vendor: make no claims about it; name one difference they can check and offer the sample list. They ask for past clients and none are published: say so plainly, name what lowers their risk, offer samples, never invent proof.
- No greeting or sign-off inside a live thread. Nothing the thread already said.
- Every fact comes from your knowledge base, the thread or their site. Anything else goes on the "New in this reply" line, because sending it makes it a commitment.
- Write down in one line what they are really asking and what they will worry about next, then answer that, not only the literal words. Put that note on the "Real question:" line of the flag; a reply without it is unfinished.
- Every cost the reply mentions carries its current number and the volume it assumes, never "at cost" alone. A figure from earlier in the thread wins only if it named its volume; otherwise re-read the live calculator or price list.
- If they quote a price benchmark, work out your total cost per outcome (fees plus tools, divided by outcomes) at a realistic volume and put it on one line above the ask when it does not beat theirs. Never say yes to "can you match or beat X" unless it does.
- Open every link before including it and check that its prices and terms do not contradict the thread. If they conflict, leave the link out. Never write "we agreed" unless they said yes in words.
- Review loop on every reply before the human sees it: a clean-context reviewer checks the exact text against the thread, the facts and the prospect's site. Fix what it finds, then use a new reviewer on the fixed text. Repeat until a round finds nothing; never stop at one round. The session that drafted the reply never reviews it, and a draft written by anyone else gets the same full research and rounds. Each round checks claims against raw page text, plain words, and the reply read as the prospect; its report gives the verdict, the plain-words result, what it was based on and a fingerprint of the exact text. The text shown for approval is word for word the text the clean round was bound to.

The closing line. Read the thread as the prospect, then the last line:

1. It fits where they are. It never asks for setup work before they have chosen you.
2. It never proposes a call. If they ask for one, a link on its own is not the answer: name two specific times, say how long the call is, and give one way to confirm (for example, reply with the time that suits). Each time is open on the live calendar, inside the hours the person taking the call has set, far enough after the send for them to prepare (your minimum lead time, for example two hours), and written in the prospect's time zone and yours. If no time is left today, offer the next business day and say so. If they named a time that is open and inside those hours, accept it in one line; if it is outside, offer the nearest two inside and say why. A booking link may follow only as the way to pick a different time, after you have opened it and checked it.
3. It never asks back what they just said, and never repeats an offer or question from your own last message that they skipped. Read your last sent reply first; drop a skipped offer or act on it. Test: could they answer "I just told you that"?
4. It asks or offers something real. "Does that help?" is empty.

Best closes, in order: offer one concrete next thing as a yes or no; check the answer to their question (the line names the specific answer it checks); ask one new, easy question.

## Part 3: send and read back

1. After the yes, you send it: the approver is never asked to work the sending tool or left holding a draft. If they ask questions before a yes, answer briefly with the reply block repeated at the top. Just before sending, confirm the composed text equals the approved text with each link once, the sending mailbox is the thread's mailbox, the recipient line is right, and the thread shows no reply already sent after their message. Then send first. Answer anything else in the message after. If the reply offered times, recheck each against now plus the minimum lead time and the live calendar just before sending; if one lapsed, propose new times and get a new yes.
2. Send the exact approved text. If one word must change, show it and get a new yes.
3. "Sending in progress" is not proof. Reload the thread and read the sent copy and its time.
4. Check whether the prospect already answered.
5. Report in two lines as its own message: who, time sent, route, any answer since. Then the notes.
6. If the send went around the normal path, fix the record so it does not show as pending.
7. If a send times out or errors while the draft still shows pending, nothing is proven: read the sent thread first and never fire a second send blind. If one send route refuses a held or edited draft, use the other.
8. They pick an offered time: open the event page first and do not book if its terms conflict with the thread. Booking for them uses only their own words for any form answers; read the confirmation back and report the time.
9. A new term or offer approved in a reply is written back to your knowledge base with the approver's words as its source.
10. If every send route fails, say so in one line with the reason, and hand the exact text and the readback to another executor.
11. A correction to one draft is a rule for every draft. In the same turn record it wherever replies are written (the knowledge base rules, this skill, your messaging notes), not only in the automation prompt. The same applies when you find your own mistake.

## Valid and Invalid

- Valid closing: "Want me to pull a short sample of the companies I'd contact for you, so you can judge the list yourself?"
- Invalid closing: "Worth a 15-minute call to go through it?" when they never asked for a call.
- Valid answer to "when are you free?": "Tomorrow at 11 AM or 2 PM Eastern (8 AM or 11 AM in your time zone) works, for 15 minutes. Reply with the one that suits you and I'll send the invite."
- Invalid answer to the same: "Happy to. Grab any time that works for you here: [link]".
- Valid report: "Sent to the prospect at 10:42 through the inbox. Read back from the thread. No answer yet."
- Invalid report: "Clicked Send, done."
- Invalid flag: the right shape, followed by three paragraphs of research notes.
